What is Present Continuous?

Actions happening RIGHT NOW ⏱️

The Present Continuous Tense talks about an action that is happening at this exact moment — while we are speaking.

Subject + am / is / are + Verb-ing I am reading. She is writing. They are playing.
Iam
He / She / Itis
You / We / Theyare

🔊 Look outside your window. What can you see people doing right now? That's Present Continuous!

Teacher Notes: 5 minutes. Point to real actions happening in the room right now ("I am talking. You are listening.") to make the concept concrete before moving to formal grammar.
Grammar Focus · Part 1

Positive Sentences ✅

👉 Use it for an action happening at this exact moment.
Look! The dog is barking. — I am doing my homework now.
👉 Use it for something happening these days (even if not this exact second).
She is learning English this year. — We are practicing for the concert this week.
SubjectFormExample
Iam + V-ingI am cooking dinner.
He / She / Itis + V-ingShe is dancing.
You / We / Theyare + V-ingThey are swimming.
Teacher Notes: 8 minutes. Emphasize the two use-cases separately with fresh examples from classroom life. TPR: point at a student doing an action and build the sentence together out loud.
Spelling Rules

How do we write verb-ing? ✍️

RuleVerbVerb-ing
Most verbs: just add -ingread, play, eatreading, playing, eating
Verb ends in silent e: drop the e, add -ingmake, write, dancemaking, writing, dancing
Short verb, one vowel + one consonant: double the last letterrun, swim, sitrunning, swimming, sitting
Verb ends in ie: change to y, add -inglie, tielying, tying
⚠️ Watch Out! runing✔ running
writeing✔ writing
Teacher Notes: 7 minutes. Write a few verbs on the board and ask students to guess which rule applies before revealing the answer — this makes the rule memorable rather than just told.
Grammar Focus · Part 2

Negative Sentences ❌

Subject + am/is/are + NOT + Verb-ing I am not sleeping. He isn't cooking. They aren't studying.
SubjectFull formShort form
II am notI'm not
He / She / ItHe is notHe isn't
You / We / TheyThey are notThey aren't
👉 Use it to say an action is NOT happening now.
She isn't watching TV — she is reading a book.
Teacher Notes: 6 minutes. Practice contrast pairs: "He is not walking, he is running" to build negative + positive together.
Grammar Focus · Part 3

Questions & Short Answers ❓

Am/Is/Are + Subject + Verb-ing ... ? Are you reading? Is she sleeping? Am I dreaming?
QuestionShort YesShort No
Are you playing?Yes, I am.No, I'm not.
Is he cooking?Yes, he is.No, he isn't.
Are they singing?Yes, they are.No, they aren't.
⚠️ Watch Out! What you are doing?✔ What are you doing?
Question words (What/Where/Why) come first, then am/is/are, then the subject.
Teacher Notes: 7 minutes. Drill the word order with hand gestures: point to yourself for the question word, sweep left-right for am/is/are + subject swap.

Reading Time

📖 A Busy Saturday

It is Saturday morning at the LittleLume Explorer Club. Lumi is painting a colorful poster for the club wall. Toby is not sitting still — he is jumping around the room with excitement because the club picnic is today! Mimi is reading a map to find the best picnic spot. "Are you packing the snacks?" Mimi asks Kiko. "Yes, I am!" Kiko answers, and she is putting apples and sandwiches into a basket. Outside, the sun is shining, and birds are singing in the trees. Everyone is getting ready for a wonderful day!

Comprehension Questions

Teacher Notes: 10 minutes. Read the story aloud first (or play TTS), then have students read silently before answering. Point out every -ing word as you read.

Final Quiz

💌 Dear Parents,

Today your child learned the Present Continuous Tense — sentences like "I am reading" or "She is playing" that describe actions happening right now. At home, try asking your child in English: "What are you doing?" during daily activities (cooking, cleaning, playing) and encourage them to answer in a full sentence. A few minutes of this each day builds real confidence!

Teacher Notes: 5 minutes. Let students self-assess honestly before the final quiz — this builds metacognitive awareness. Send the Dear Parents note home if printing.
