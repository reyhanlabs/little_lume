Page 4 REVIEW 1

🔵 Simple Present

What is Simple Present?

Simple Present is the tense we use for things that happen again and again, or things that are always true.

When do we use it?

🔁 Daily routines — I brush my teeth every morning.
🎯 Habits — She always reads before bed.
🌍 Facts — The sun rises in the east.
🗓️ Schedules — The bus leaves at 7 a.m.

Formula

➕ Affirmative Subject + Verb (+s/es) + ...
➖ Negative Subject + do/does + not + Verb + ...
❓ Interrogative Do/Does + Subject + Verb + ...?

Subject + Verb Chart

SubjectVerb formExample
I / You / We / TheyVerb (base form)They play football.
He / She / ItVerb + s / esShe plays football.

🤖 Robo's rule: He, She, It → add "s" or "es" to the verb!

Signal Words ⏰

alwaysusuallyoften sometimesevery dayevery morning on Mondays

Example Sentences

  1. I wake up at six o'clock.
  2. My mother cooks breakfast every day.
  3. We go to school on Mondays.
  4. He plays basketball after school.
  5. She doesn't like spicy food.
  6. The cat sleeps on the sofa.
  7. Do you brush your teeth every night?
  8. My father drives to work.
  9. They don't watch TV in the morning.
  10. Does she read every day?
🤖 Grammar Tip: For "he/she/it", if the verb ends in -o, -ch, -sh, -x, -s → add -es (go → goes, watch → watches).
🤖 Grammar Tip: Don't forget "do/does" in questions and negatives — never add "s" twice! ("She doesn't likes" is WRONG.)

👩‍🏫 Teacher Notes

  • Estimated Time: 15 minutes
  • Objective: Recall the form and use of Simple Present.
  • Classroom Tip: Have students clap once for "he/she/it" verbs that need -s.
Page 5 REVIEW 2

🟣 Simple Past

What is Simple Past?

Simple Past is the tense we use for actions that are already finished — they happened before now.

When do we use it?

✅ Completed actions — I finished my homework.
📅 Past events — We visited Grandma last week.
🏁 Finished activities — She watched a movie yesterday.

Formula

➕ Affirmative Subject + Verb-ed (or V2) + ...
➖ Negative Subject + did + not + Verb (base form) + ...
❓ Interrogative Did + Subject + Verb (base form) + ...?

Regular Verbs (Verb + ed)

Base VerbPast Form

watchwatched

Example: I played football yesterday. She studied for the test last night.

Signal Words ⏰

🤖 Grammar Tip: Irregular verbs don't follow the "+ed" rule — you just have to remember them, like magic spells! Practice a few every day. ✨
🤖 Grammar Tip: In negatives and questions, the verb goes back to its base form: "Did you go?" NOT "Did you went?"

👩‍🏫 Teacher Notes

  • Estimated Time: 15 minutes
  • Objective: Recall the form and use of Simple Past, including irregular verbs.
  • Classroom Tip: Turn the irregular verb table into a quick chant or a "verb bingo" for extra practice.
Page 6 COMPARE

Simple Present vs Simple Past ⚖️

🔵 Simple Present🟣 Simple Past
🎯 PurposeRoutines, habits, factsFinished actions
⏰ TimeNow / always / regularlyBefore now (finished)
✍️ Verb FormVerb / Verb+sVerb+ed / V2 (irregular)
🔔 Signal Wordsalways, usually, every dayyesterday, last week, ago
💬 ExampleI walk to school.I walked to school.
Remember! 🌟

If it happens again and again → use Simple Present.
If it already finished → use Simple Past.
Look for the signal word — it's your best clue! 🔍

👩‍🏫 Teacher Notes

  • Estimated Time: 8 minutes
  • Objective: Directly contrast the two tenses side by side.
  • Classroom Tip: Ask students to cover one column and quiz a partner using the other.
